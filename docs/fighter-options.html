Fighter Playtest Options

Concrete ways to answer the open questions in the April fighter outline — enough to start flying, and specific enough to argue with.

Against FIGHTERS AND SMALL CRAFT, notes and outline, Apr 2026 · with the 4‑25‑2026 playtest notes · and the 16 stat cards embedded in that document

Every option below is playable as written. Where the published v2.6 rules or the V41 builder already settle something, that is said plainly rather than re-asked — about a third of the outline turns out to be in print already.

One option in each set is marked start here. That is not a verdict, it is where we would begin so that playtesting has a baseline to deviate from.

First, the numbers hold up

Before any of the open questions: the dogfight model in the outline was checked against the playtest samples recorded at the bottom of that same document. It predicts them.

The pace you observed is the pace the maths predicts

Six fighters at DFR 1‑3 against dodge 1‑2: each fighter hits on 50%, each hit kills through a 67% failed dodge, so a flight of six lands 2.0 kills per phase and wipes six opposing fighters in 3.0 phases. The three sample dogfights ran 2, 4 and 5 phases — mean 3.7.

And three combat phases make a round, so “two flights in a dogfight will likely resolve their combat in a round” is not a guess in the notes — it is what the rating scale actually produces. The DFR and Dodge numbers are doing what you intended them to do.

The three that block everything

Q1

d6 or the coloured dice?

Blocking
“Standard 6-sided dice are used. The colored dice impose constraints and lack granularity.”

You are right about the granularity, and it is worth seeing how right: the coloured dice offer two hit probabilities — 83% for red, yellow and green, 67% for blue — because only the blank faces differ. A d6 roll-under offers five, at 17/33/50/67/83%. The DFR ladder needs all five.

But the binary in the question may be false. When a ship shoots at a flight it rolls its own printed attack dice, which are coloured, under E12.4.3 and E12.4.4. When fighters shoot at each other they roll d6. The two systems never meet inside a single roll — the seam already exists in the published rules, and neither die type has to be translated into the other.

AKeep them separate, as they already areStart here

Fighter‑vs‑fighter and fighter survival roll d6. Anything a starship’s guns do stays coloured. The end‑of‑battle casualty roll becomes a d6 too, resolving the one place the outline crosses over.

At the table You never hold both die types for the same decision.

BColoured dice throughout

Express DFR as a die colour and a face threshold. Costs you three of the five DFR steps, so the Frazi, Starfury and Nial collapse toward each other.

At the table One die type, a flatter roster.

Cd6 everywhere, including ships shooting fighters

Replace the coloured attack dice with a d6 to‑hit whenever the target is a small craft. Uniform, but it detaches a ship’s anti‑fighter fire from its own printed weapon chart.

At the table Simplest to teach; loses the weapon’s character.

Q2

How do ships kill fighters?

Blocking

You wrote “use COA 1 for now since its an existing game mechanic.” Two things sharpen that. E12.4.2 already says the firing player is “not required to assign specific weapon mounts to a single small target; only the volley or flight is required” — COA 1’s pooling, already in print. And COA 2 cannot be run on the current cards: it needs a light/medium/heavy weight class per fighter, and the cards give a Structure number instead.

ACOA 1, pooledStart here

Total the damage, halve non‑PD fire first per E10.2.3, divide by the fighter’s Structure, remove that many. Deterministic once the dice are down.

At the table Fast, and already how E12.4.2 targets a flight.

BCOA 1 with dodge, defender chooses

Fighters dodge the incoming dice and the owning player picks which are cancelled. Adds a decision to the defender, and a roll per die.

At the table More agency, noticeably slower on a big volley.

CCOA 2, hit classes

Light/Medium/Heavy hits destroy fighters by weight class. Needs a weight class added to every card before it can be played at all.

At the table Lumpier and quicker — but blocked on new data.

Q3

Are the six card fighters a roster, or a calibration set?

Blocking

Every card is a Babylon 5 airframe — Starfury, Thunderbolt, Frazi, Nial, Sentri. Not one of StarForce Commander’s own factions has a fighter among them. Separately, and regardless of the answer: nothing on a card prices a flight, and the builder explicitly excludes fighters from the hangar’s cost.

ACalibration setStart here

Treat them the way the cross‑franchise hulls are already treated — clearly marked as fan designs, fully playable, kept out of the printed roster. SFC‑native craft follow later.

Needs Point values, so flights can be bought.

BReskin them

Keep the numbers, give each airframe a Union / Vallari / Aurelian / Pirate identity. The calibration survives; the setting stays its own.

Needs Six names and a faction each.

CGeneric classes

Publish them by role — interceptor, multi‑role, bomber, recon — and let any faction field them, the way the outline’s own “SMALL CRAFT TYPES” list is written.

Needs Nothing new; loses the flavour.

Where a number is simply missing

Q4

How many attacks does a load buy?

Number missing

“When ordinance is expended, the counter is flipped” gives the trigger and no quantity. The cards hint at the answer: BASIC is always the weakest strike on every airframe — 1×1 on the Starfury and Sentri, meaning it hits a ship only on a natural 1, for a single point. That is exactly what a fighter with its ordnance gone should look like.

AOne strike, then flip to BASICStart here

The card’s configuration is the loaded side; BASIC is the reverse. Fits “a good initial strike and then a reduction in capability,” and needs no new numbers at all.

At the table The double‑sided counter is solved by the cards you have.

BTwo strikes per load

Flip after the second attack. Keeps a strike flight relevant for two passes instead of one, at the cost of a marker to track the first shot.

At the table Carriers matter less; flights linger.

CLoadout‑specific counts

Long‑range anti‑ship torpedoes get one, short‑range anti‑fighter missiles get several. Truest to the loadout list — and the one option that needs a new number on every card.

At the table Most texture, most bookkeeping.

Q5

Launch and landing rates

Answered by the builder

Both lines in the outline are placeholders. The V41 builder already fills them: LNCH at 2 points — “one launch bay represents the ability to launch a fighter unit” — and LAND at 1 point for recovery, with HNGR at 1 point holding “a full fighter unit (generally 2‑12 craft).” We would take that as the rule: one flight out per launch bay per phase, one in per landing bay, replacing J8.1.2’s single shuttle for carriers rather than adding to it.

One conflict to settle: the builder calls the landing bay LAND; rulebook E8.4.6 calls it LNDG.

Where the published rules force the question

Q12

Does a flight launch as one craft, or as six?

Rules conflict

H6.15.4 gives every searching ship an extra detection roll per small craft launched, and the Aurelian reference card prints it as “+1 Roll per Small Craft Launched.” If a six‑fighter flight counts as six launches, a cloaked carrier is effectively forbidden to launch — six extra detection dice to every hunter in range.

AA flight is one launchStart here

One extra detection roll however many fighters go out. Consistent with the flight being the unit everywhere else — movement, range, and targeting all measure the flight, not the fighter.

At the table Cloaked carriers are viable.

BEvery fighter counts

Six launches, six rolls. Cloak and carrier become mutually exclusive doctrines — which may be exactly the intent, since a cloaked carrier is a strong thing to have.

At the table Cloak is for raiders, not carriers.

CLaunching breaks the cloak outright

No dice: the carrier decloaks to open the bay. Simplest to adjudicate and the most thematic — the bay doors are the tell.

At the table One clean decision instead of a dice pool.

Q18

Jamming is doing an enormous amount of work

Untested

Jamming runs 5 to 8 across the six airframes, and under E10.2.2 a target’s jamming is added to the actual range of any non‑point‑defense attack — pushing the volley into a worse bracket or off the chart entirely. It is not a to‑hit modifier; it is a range shift, and it is easy to under‑read.

A Nial at jamming 8 is close to untouchable by a main battery. Only point‑defense weapons, which ignore the penalty under E12.4.3, can answer it. That may be exactly right — it makes PD mounts the anti‑fighter system, which matches F1.20 — but it is the single most consequential number on the cards and nothing has tested it.

AExactly as E10.2.2 is publishedStart here

Full jamming added to range against non‑PD fire. Play it straight, measure what it does to fighter survivability, and change it only with numbers in hand.

At the table Main batteries stop bothering with fighters.

BHalve it against fighters

A Nial reads as jamming 4 to a non‑PD volley. Keeps the ranking between airframes while letting a desperate cruiser still shoot back.

At the table Fighters are hard to hit, not immune.

CCap the shift at one bracket

Jamming can worsen the range band by one step and no more. Predictable, and keeps every weapon on its own printed chart.

At the table Easiest to reason about mid‑game.

Two packages to fly

Rather than testing nineteen variables, two coherent rulesets. Both are complete enough to play a whole battle; they differ on the two axes we think are genuinely uncertain.

Package A — Straight as written, read literally
  • d6 for fighters, coloured dice for ships (Q1‑A)
  • COA 1 pooled, non‑PD halved first (Q2‑A)
  • One strike, then flip to BASIC (Q4‑A)
  • A flight is one launch for cloak detection (Q12‑A)
  • Jamming exactly as E10.2.2 (Q18‑A)
  • One flight per LNCH box out, per LAND box in (Q5)
Package B — Ships fight back same core, two dials turned
  • Everything in Package A, except:
  • Jamming halved against fighters (Q18‑B)
  • Two strikes per load before the flip (Q4‑B)

What the pair is testing. Package A is the outline read literally, and its likely failure mode is that fighters become untouchable by anything but point defense while a strike flight is spent after one pass. Package B loosens exactly those two things. If A plays well, B is unnecessary; if fighters feel oppressive or disposable, B says which of the two is responsible.

What we would ask you to rule on

  • Q1 — is the d6/coloured seam where we put it, or somewhere else?
  • Q2 — COA 1 pooled, confirmed? And is COA 2 worth adding weight classes for later?
  • Q3 — calibration set or roster, and what does a flight cost?
  • Q4 — is BASIC the expended side of the card, or a third loadout?
  • Q12 — one launch or six, for a cloaked carrier?
  • Q18 — is a jamming‑8 fighter meant to be that hard for a main battery to hit?
  • And the small one: LAND or LNDG?

Everything else in the outline we can build from what is already published, or from a default that costs nothing to change later.
